The recipe

Method & ratios

Tajarin is Piedmont's answer to fresh egg pasta — thin ribbons, a rich egg-yolk color, and a springy-but-structured bite. The dough is yolk-only, no whole eggs and no added water. Traditionally the flour was just "00" — nothing else. Substituting some of it with semolina is a newer refinement: it gives the fine-cut ribbons more backbone so they hold their shape without tearing or cracking, where pure "00" alone is silkier but a bit slacker. Use the semolina toggle in the calculator above to switch between the two — 70/30 "00"/semolina for more tooth, or all-"00" for the traditional version.

Target texture: pliable but structured — springy, not stiff, and able to hold a fine 2mm cut without tearing.

  1. Place the weighed egg yolk and both flours in a mixing bowl.
  2. Knead 8–10 minutes minimum. The dough looks dry and shaggy at first — keep going. It becomes smooth and pliable as the gluten hydrates. Don't stop early.
  3. Rest, wrapped tightly, 30–60 minutes at room temperature before rolling. This redistributes moisture through the dough and prevents the wet-then-cracky problem of rolling too soon.
  4. Roll to the thinnest or near-thinnest setting on your pasta machine.
  5. Cut at 2mm width for classic tajarin.

Adjusting the dough

Too wet: reduce the yolk ratio in 5g increments on your next batch — or knead and rest longer before judging; a lot of the wet-shaggy feeling resolves on its own with time.

Too dry or not pliable: add water carefully — a few drops at a time can take a surprisingly long time to bring a dry dough together, so use your own judgment rather than counting drops. If it's especially dry or stiff, start with about ½ teaspoon rather than single drops, knead it in fully, and reassess before adding more. Never a whole egg — that dilutes the yolk-forward flavor.

A note on food safety

Mixed dough covered at room temperature is low-risk and standard practice for the 30–60 minute rest. If you need to rest it longer than about 2 hours, move it to the fridge.

Base ratio, for reference: yolk weight = 60% of total flour weight (a 55–65% range works; 60% is the sweet spot), regardless of how that flour is split. This batch's worked example, with the semolina toggle on: 126g yolk to 210g total flour (147g "00" + 63g semolina) — exactly what the calculator above returns at 2 portions.

Storing your pasta

Fresh, dried, or frozen

Fresh tajarin is best cooked the day it's made, but it keeps well with a little planning. The key distinction is whether your pasta is partially dried (still pliable) or fully dried (brittle, snaps cleanly like store-bought).

Partially dried — still pliable

  • Paper bag at room temp: 1–2 days max
  • Refrigerate in a lightly floured container: up to 3 days
  • Risk of mold in humid conditions — when in doubt, refrigerate

Fully dried — brittle

  • Allow 24–48 hours depending on humidity and thickness
  • Paper bag in a cool dry pantry: 4–8 weeks
  • Airtight jar or tin after drying: 2–3 months or longer

Freezing nests

  • Dust nests with rice flour before forming — its fine dry particles prevent sticking far better than semolina (use semolina if that's all you have)
  • Flash-freeze on a tray in a single layer for 1–2 hours until firm
  • Transfer to a freezer bag — they'll stay separate
  • Keeps well for up to 2 months with no noticeable quality loss

Cooking from frozen

  • Drop frozen nests directly into boiling salted water — no thawing needed
  • Add 1–2 minutes to your usual cook time
  • Taste early — fine ribbons cook fast even from frozen
